## Recap of Tuesday (10.27) [00:00-04:12]
- Earnings move stock prices two ways. A surprise at the announcement causes a quick price jump. That's not today's focus.
- Today is about the long-run buy-and-hold return. In theory, return with no new info is just pay for bearing risk.
- CAPM says expected return only depends on market risk. Fama-French 3-factor adds size and value. Neither model has earnings or profitability in it.
- So the question is whether earnings predict alpha (actual return minus the Fama-French expected return). If they do, that's return you get without extra risk.
- The whole analysis is one regression. Regress alpha (abnormal return) on earnings.

- Leave the estimation window numbers alone. Event window is day +10 to day +50 (2 weeks to 10 weeks after the announcement), so the surprise is already priced in and it's before the next announcement.

## Result and takeaway [1:12:39-1:15:44]
- ROE comes out positive and significant. Buying high-ROE stocks seems to earn abnormal return.
- This isn't new. Ball and Brown (the first empirical accounting paper) showed prices keep drifting after the announcement, up for good earnings and down for bad. This is called post-earnings announcement drift and it's held up for 50+ years.
- In 2015 Fama and French added a profitability factor (their 5-factor model). Under that view, the ROE return is pay for risk, not free money.
